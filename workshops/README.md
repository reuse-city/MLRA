# Participatory Co-Design Workshops

This directory documents the co-design workshops, participatory working group sessions, agendas, and community feedback throughout the **Material Literacy and Resource Assessment (MLRA)** project.

---

## 🎯 Purpose of the Participatory Working Group

Knowledge about local materials, repair techniques, and circular resource management is inherently distributed, experiential, and context-specific. The working group brings together practitioners from open workshops, repair cafés, makerspaces, circular economy initiatives, and educational designers across the Global Innovation Gathering (GIG) network and partner communities.

The goal is to collaboratively shape the curriculum, review didactic approaches, and ensure that the resulting learning modules are genuinely useful, adaptable, and grounded in community practice.

---

## 🗓️ Phase 1 Working Sessions (November 2026)

Three focused online sessions are planned for Phase 1 to lay the foundation for course authoring and platform integration:

### Session 1: Community Needs Assessment & Material Practices
* **Focus:** Mapping the real-world material flows, diagnostics needs, and pain points in grassroots repair cafés, makerspaces, and community workshops.
* **Key Questions:**
  * Which materials present the highest barriers to identification, testing, and safe handling?
  * What diagnostic practices and heuristics do repairers and makers already rely on?
  * What are the main knowledge gaps when deciding whether an item/material should be repaired, repurposed, upcycled, or recycled?
* **Outcomes:** A prioritized inventory of material topics and diagnostic workflows to be covered in the curriculum.

### Session 2: Didactic Approaches for Online & Hybrid Learning
* **Focus:** How to effectively teach hands-on, tacit material skills through a low-barrier online format.
* **Key Questions:**
  * How do we structure self-paced learning paths so that learners can practice diagnostics offline in their local spaces?
  * What multimedia formats (short videos, visual decision trees, downloadable test cards) work best across varied bandwidth environments?
  * How can peer review and community reflection be integrated into the Global Innovation Academy (GIA) platform?
* **Outcomes:** Didactic guidelines, template designs for practical exercises, and media requirements.

### Session 3: Curriculum Validation & Ideation for Local Adaptations
* **Focus:** Validating the core learning pathways and establishing principles for local translation and contextual adaptation.
* **Key Questions:**
  * How does the draft MLRA curriculum align with regional material realities (Global South and Global Majority contexts)?
  * What guidelines are needed so a workshop in Nairobi, Berlin, or São Paulo can adapt the course to local waste streams and tools?
  * How will participating spaces pilot and test the course in Phase 3 (March–April 2027)?
* **Outcomes:** Validated course syllabus, draft local adaptation framework, and an initial pool of pilot testing partners.
